Succession planner

Direct transfer of control at death with as little probate exposure, administrative delay or estate-challenge risk as the vehicle allows — while keeping management and purpose continuous. Flags only. Not estate-planning advice.

People often have different priorities. Some want protection while they are alive. Others want certainty on death. Many want a balance. When you compare structures, look at ownership, tax, governance and stewardship — and at how control actually moves.

Succession objective

Keep the next people able to act. Reduce the chance that a grant of probate is the only key to the land or the enterprise. Do not confuse “the will says so” with “the vehicle already has a successor.”

Assessment criteria on every card

  • Passes through estate? Do the assets form part of the deceased estate, or can control move outside that framework?
  • Probate exposure. Is a grant typically needed before successors can act? Could delay affect land management?
  • Control transfer mechanism. Trustee succession, corporate director succession, appointor succession, shareholder succession, or membership / governance succession.
  • Delay risk. Can the next generation act immediately, or is there a legal bottleneck?
  • Estate challenge exposure. Could a dispute about the estate disturb the intended outcome? How much does the arrangement lean on a will?
  • Complexity and cost. Simplicity of setup, ongoing administration, legal complexity of the succession clauses.
  • Multi-generational continuity. Fit for preserving land, an enterprise, family stewardship or community purpose across more than one generation.

▣A charity or community land trust with an asset lock cannot be a substitute for a family discretionary trust.

If family succession is the primary objective

Prioritise vehicles that show a written control path, reduced dependence on probate alone, continuity of day-to-day management, and governance that a later generation can actually use. Then read the Compare room columns for Succession & continuity.

Important limit

No structure can guarantee that disputes, challenges, delays or court involvement will never occur. Outcomes depend on trust deeds, constitutions, governance documents, estate planning, taxation and applicable Australian laws. Structure Lab AU is educational only and does not provide legal, tax, financial or estate-planning advice.

Typical watch-fors

  • Appointor of a family trust dies and the deed is silent or points at the legal personal representative.
  • Corporate trustee has one director and no alternate.
  • SMSF has no current binding death benefit nomination, or the nomination has lapsed.
  • Land is in personal names and the only path is the will plus probate.
  • Purpose entity has no documented responsible-person succession.
  • A covenant is treated as if it were a holding vehicle.

Work order is almost always: read the deed and title → write the will / BDBN / appointor clause → then consider any movement of land. See the timeline.
