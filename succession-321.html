Succession 321

Room 12. A twelve-station walk for individual and family, business and community continuity. The rhythm is Barefoot — date night, buckets, Mojo, Fearless Folder, leave a legacy. The documents are Australian, with NSW witnessing rules called out. Prepared October 2026. General planning information, not legal, tax or financial advice.

Succession law is state-based. Engage a wills-and-estates lawyer, a registered tax agent and an insurance adviser before acting. Structure flags stay in the succession planner. This room is the household operating plan.

▣A charity or community land trust with an asset lock cannot be a substitute for a family discretionary trust. 3-2-1 here means three buckets, two successions (who owns / who runs), one folder someone else can open.

01 — Why succession planning matters

It is the deliberate transfer of roles, assets, responsibilities, relationships and values. The four layers interact: a family-business transition is also a wealth transition and a retirement plan. Pape’s step 9 asks how you want to be remembered. That is the same job, in plainer clothes. Barefoot Steps.

The risk of doing nothing is documented. Grant Thornton’s 2025 family-business report put documented succession plans at 19 per cent of Australian family businesses. Deloitte found that while most families claim some form of plan, only about half call it thorough. PwC’s survey describes uneven planning, skills gaps and delayed transitions.

  • Grant Thornton 2025 Family Business Report
  • Deloitte — Succession is getting real
  • PwC Australia Family Business Survey

Core rule at every layer: plan the transition before you need it. Death, incapacity, dispute or resignation is the worst time to design one.

02 — Date night before documents

Step 1 is a monthly sitting where the steps actually get done. Use it as the family meeting. One decision. Write it down. Include the next generation early enough that the first conversation is not the funeral. Detail and the nine-step order: room 10.

03 — Buckets: what must survive you

Blow runs the week. Mojo is three months of living expenses so the household is not selling shares to pay for a funeral. Grow is super, the house, the business, the land — the assets whose control path is usually not the will. If Mojo is in an account only you can see, it is not Mojo. Serviette: step 2.

04 — Individual papers

Incapacity can strike at any age. For a NSW resident the working set is:

  • Will — estate assets. Keep current.
  • Enduring power of attorney — financial and legal decisions. Made with capacity.
  • Enduring guardian — health and lifestyle. Effect on loss of capacity. Eligible witness required. Legal Aid NSW, NSW Local Court.
  • Advance care directive — NSW has no statutory scheme. A common-law directive can bind if voluntary, capable and clear. Advance Care Planning Australia, NSW Health.
  • Super death-benefit nomination — super generally does not follow the will. ATO.

05 — Fearless Folder

Pape’s folder holds the will, powers of attorney, binding nomination, account list, logins, funeral note and a final letter. Originals in a bolted safe. Scans in a dedicated email with app two-factor authentication. Do not put funeral wishes only in the will. Full NSW checklist: room 11. His column: Fearless Folder.

06 — Super and SMSF control

His step 5 is super to 15 per cent. That is a savings habit. The succession habit is the nomination, and for an SMSF the deed: who becomes trustee, who becomes director of the corporate trustee, and whether a binding nomination has lapsed. If super is paid to the estate, the will governs from there — and probate timing comes with it. Caps and tax: a registered tax agent, against ATO caps.

07 — Family: wealth, care, values

Conflict usually starts because expectations were never said out loud. Practical pieces:

  • Annual family meeting — the date night, with the next generation in the room before they inherit a surprise.
  • A family charter. Non-binding. Values, how decisions are made, how someone joins or leaves the business, how a dispute is handled.
  • Wealth transfer options — testamentary trusts, family trusts, super, gifts. Each has a tax and control trade-off. Not a DIY choice.
  • Care: who looks after whom, and how it is funded.
  • Blended families: second marriages need explicit documents so assets do not walk away from the children you intended.
  • Successor education: literacy, governance exposure, outside experience, before control.
  • A dispute path agreed in advance — family council, independent chair, mediation clause.

08 — Trust control is the succession

Trust assets may not pass under a will. Read the deed for appointor, principal or guardian succession, trustee replacement, corporate-trustee shares and directorships, loan accounts and unpaid present entitlements. A silent appointor clause that points at the legal personal representative puts you back in probate. This is the job of the succession planner and the structure cards.

09 — Business: who runs it

Ownership and management are separable. Plan both. Management: name successors three to five years out and give them real delegations and a P&L. Write down the key-person dependencies — systems, relationships, approvals, passwords. Emergency plan: who is interim manager if a principal is gone tonight.

10 — Business: who owns it

  • Buy-sell agreement. What happens to an interest on death, disability, trauma, retirement or resignation. Often called a will for the business. FS Advice note (PDF).
  • Funding. Life, TPD and trauma so survivors are not forced to sell or borrow. Key-person cover is revenue protection, a different job from buy-sell funding. Hudson Financial Planning, AIA ownership guide (PDF).
  • Valuation method agreed in the document, so a death is not a fire-sale argument.
  • CGT flags, not a calculation. Small-business concessions — 15-year exemption, 50 per cent active-asset reduction, retirement exemption, rollover — can matter on a sale or a transfer, and sometimes in a deceased estate. Eligibility is the accountant’s job. ATO concessions, death and the concessions.
  • Structures and guarantees. A trust or company can help or block the transfer. Personal guarantees need a release path.
  • Options. Family, management buyout, employee ownership, trade sale, wind-down. Evaluate them. Do not assume the eldest child.

11 — Community

Volunteer organisations fail when one person holds the logins. Every role gets a deputy. Every deputy is in training. Avoid the same three people rotating every office.

  • Handover kit per role: calendar, contacts, where the accounts are.
  • Staggered terms for continuity. Term limits for renewal.
  • NSW incorporated associations: committee members must act for the association’s benefit, with care and diligence, and mind financial viability. At least three adult members. NSW Government, Associations Incorporation Act 2009.
  • ACNC registered charities: Responsible People must act honestly, with reasonable care, in the charity’s best interests. Governance Standard 5, Governance for good.
  • Emergency line: if the treasurer disappears tomorrow, who can sign, where are the accounts, who tells the members.
  • Stakeholder map: funder, landlord, regulator — each with a backup contact.

12 — Map, timeline, team

Five moves, at every layer: inventory, decide, document, prepare, review. Prepare includes a test — the key person is gone tomorrow; what breaks?
